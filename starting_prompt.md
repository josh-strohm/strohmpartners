Build a clean, modern, 3-page professional website for **Strohm Partners LLC**, an AI automation agency that builds the digital systems modern businesses depend on.

Strohm Partners delivers three connected service areas under one umbrella:
- Custom website design, development, and on-site automation.
- Social media and content automation — systems that produce and publish on-brand content consistently.
- Business process automation — internal workflows that eliminate manual work and connect the tools a business already uses.

The site should make clear that these are not three separate offerings bolted together — they're one practice that thinks about a business as a system.

Do not name or imply any sub-brands. There is only one brand on the site: **Strohm Partners**. Use first-person plural throughout.

## Tone
- Modern, sharp, slightly technical without being inaccessible.
- Confident but never boastful.
- Honest about being an AI-forward agency without making AI the headline.

## Truthfulness
- Absolutely no fabricated case studies, client names, logos, testimonials, ratings, stats, or revenue numbers.
- Where social proof would normally appear, design the slot but leave it clearly marked as a placeholder for future content. Better to under-promise than to invent.
- Talk about capability, philosophy, and process — not invented history.

## The Interactive Layer (important)
The site itself is the portfolio. Every interactive element below should feel intentional, polished, and demonstrate the kind of craft a prospective client is hiring us for. Interactivity should never feel like decoration — it should make the site more useful, more memorable, or more revealing of how we think.

Use the following interactive elements throughout the site, distributed across the three pages so no single page feels overloaded:

1. **Live cursor-reactive hero** — the homepage hero has a subtle, performant background that reacts to cursor movement (a flowing gradient mesh, a particle field, or a soft parallax grid — pick whichever feels most premium). It should be smooth on mobile, never janky, and degrade gracefully on low-power devices.

2. **The Systems Diagram** — on the homepage, an interactive diagram visualizing how websites, content automation, and business process automation connect inside a real business. Nodes are clickable; clicking a node animates a path of data flowing between systems and reveals a short explanation in a side panel. This is the visual centerpiece of the site.

3. **"See it work" mini-demos** — embedded in the What We Build page, three small live, interactive demos, one per service pillar:
   - **Web automation demo:** a sample lead capture form that, when submitted with placeholder data, triggers an animated sequence on-screen showing the lead being qualified, routed, and acknowledged in real time. No data is actually sent anywhere — it's a visualized simulation of what we build for clients.
   - **Content automation demo:** an interactive widget where a user types a topic or selects a brand voice, and the UI animates through the steps a content system would take — drafting, formatting, scheduling, publishing — ending in a mocked-up social post preview. Outputs can be canned/scripted; the point is to show the *shape* of the system.
   - **Process automation demo:** an interactive flowchart where the user can drag-connect a few sample nodes (e.g., "new client" → "send contract" → "create folder" → "notify team") and watch the flow animate end-to-end. Pre-built, constrained, and impossible to break.

4. **Theme toggle done well** — a thoughtful light/dark toggle with a smooth, custom transition (not the default flicker). The toggle itself should feel like a small piece of craft.

5. **Magnetic CTAs and refined micro-interactions** — primary buttons subtly attract the cursor, hover states are considered, focus states are visible and beautiful, and section reveals on scroll use restrained, purposeful motion. Nothing should feel decorative — every micro-interaction should reinforce that we care about detail.

6. **Live cost-of-manual-work calculator** — on the What We Build page, an interactive calculator where a visitor inputs a few simple values (hours per week spent on a manual task, hourly cost, weeks per year) and the UI animates the annual cost back to them. Frames the conversation around ROI without inventing client stats. Honest math, no fake outputs.

7. **Interactive contact form** — the contact form is itself a piece of design work: multi-step, friendly, with smooth transitions between questions, inline validation, and a satisfying confirmation state. The form is also a quiet demo of what good lead capture looks like.

8. **Keyboard-navigable everything** — every interactive element above is fully usable with keyboard alone and respects `prefers-reduced-motion` for users who opt out of animation. Accessibility is part of the showcase, not an afterthought.

## Pages (3 total)

### 1. Home
- Cursor-reactive hero with positioning statement, sub-line, and one primary CTA.
- A short "what we do" section introducing the three service pillars as a connected system, not a menu.
- The interactive Systems Diagram as the centerpiece — visually anchoring the idea that we treat a business as one system.
- A "how we work" section with 3-4 process steps explained plainly, revealed with subtle scroll-triggered motion.
- A point-of-view section: a paragraph or two on how we think about AI, automation, and the modern small business.
- A founder-credibility section (real, no inflated numbers).
- Final CTA pointing to the contact page, using a magnetic button.

### 2. What We Build
A single, well-structured services page covering all three pillars with equal weight, with each pillar paired with its corresponding "see it work" mini-demo:

1. **Websites & Web Automation**
   - Custom-designed, built for speed and conversion.
   - Lead capture, qualification, routing, and follow-up handled automatically.
   - Integrations with the tools the client already uses.
   - Paired with the live lead-capture simulation demo.

2. **Content & Social Media Automation**
   - End-to-end content systems: planning, drafting, scheduling, publishing.
   - On-brand output across channels without a full marketing team.
   - Built so the business owns the system, not just the output.
   - Paired with the interactive content pipeline widget.

3. **Business Process Automation**
   - Workflows that eliminate manual handoffs.
   - Connecting CRMs, inboxes, spreadsheets, and back-office tools into one coherent system.
   - Internal operations that quietly run themselves.
   - Paired with the drag-connect flowchart demo.

   For each: the promise, the typical symptoms before we're hired, what we deliver, and what an ideal-fit client looks like. No invented numbers.

   The cost-of-manual-work calculator lives between the second and third pillars, framed as "What is this actually costing you?"

### 3. Contact
- The multi-step interactive contact form as the centerpiece.
- Plain language about what happens after the form is submitted.
- Optional FAQ section addressing common objections (timelines, ownership of work, what "AI-powered" actually means here), implemented as smooth, accessible accordions.

## Design
- Clean, modern, slightly editorial. Strong typographic hierarchy.
- Restrained palette with one confident accent color used to draw attention to interactive elements.
- Tasteful, purposeful motion. Nothing decorative.
- Mobile-first, fully responsive. Every interactive element must have a thoughtful mobile equivalent — not just a desktop effect that's been hidden.
- WCAG 2.1 AA accessible. Visible focus states. Full keyboard support. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Visual rhythm consistent across all three pages.
- Performance is non-negotiable: the site must feel instant. Heavy interactive elements should lazy-load and never block the initial paint.

## Copy Rules
- Lead with the reader's situation, then our response.
- Banned: "revolutionary," "transform," "unlock," "synergy," "leverage" as a verb, "game-changing."
- Reading level: educated but not academic.
- Every page ends with a clear, single next step.
- Interactive demos should be introduced with one short, honest line — never overhyped. The interaction does the talking.

The finished site should feel like a serious modern firm whose work you can sample before you ever talk to them — confident, honest, technically excellent, and uninterested in pretending to be bigger than it is.
